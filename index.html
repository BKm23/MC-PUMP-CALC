About this tool

Sizes a medium-consistency (MC) centrifugal stock pump, its discharge piping, the drive motor and the VFD using the AHLSTROM / Kamyr procedure built on Bodenheimer's friction-loss formula. All calculation happens in your browser — no server, no data upload. Switch units with the toggle at the top right.

Friction gradient: hf = 4.72 × F × Q0.15 × C2.5 / D1.3 (ft head per 100 ft; Q in US GPM, D in inches, C in % BD).
F = F1·F2·F3 where F1 = stock quality, F2 = pH factor, F3 = 1.6 − 0.006·T(°F).
Flow: Q (GPM) = 15 × Production (air-dry short t/d) / Consistency (%) (16.51 × Production in air-dry metric t/d).

All losses as equivalent length. Bends, tees, Ys, control valves, mixers, meters and reducers are all handled as equivalent length in feet — added to the straight run length, then multiplied by the friction gradient hf. This matches the older AHLSTROM “Mixer Pressure Calculations” convention. Note: for fixed-pressure devices (e.g. an orifice with a stated psi drop), convert the fixed pressure to feet of head, add it as a separate static term or use a section with a very short pipe length; treating a fixed-pressure drop as equivalent length would make it scale with hf, which is incorrect.

Anti-pulsation rule. Minimum MC pipeline velocity is 0.5 ft/s (0.15 m/s); below it the plug flow breaks into stick/slip pulsation that damages pipe supports. This sets the VFD's minimum safe speed.

Reference. Method from AHLSTROM / Kamyr inter-office memo "MC Pumps & Pipelines" by K. Serenius, 19 April 1990 (Section 2 “Friction Loss Calculations” and Pumps Selection Procedure N0095). Friction correlation: V. B. Bodenheimer, TAPPI. Vendor pump curves and the maximum power along the curve must always be checked before final selection.

Validity. Bodenheimer's formula is a turbulent-regime correlation; treat results below roughly 1 ft/s with caution. NPSH for MC pumps is governed by the integral degassing system — confirm with the pump vendor.
